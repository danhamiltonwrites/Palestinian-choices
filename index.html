Palestinian Choices: Mandate Era to Today

Much commentary on the ideological left, and often in mainstream media, describes the conflict in terms borrowed from Marxist theory: Israel as the oppressor and the Palestinians as the oppressed. In that frame, Palestinian actions appear mainly as “resistance,” a reaction to being oppressed. This document tests that frame against the record. It lists key decisions made over the past century by Palestinian and Arab leaders and their allies, including rejected compromises, maximalist demands, propaganda, war, terrorism and subterfuge.

Those decisions still shape the conflict, and today’s impasse is hard to understand without them. Some entries record choices by Arab states and Iran, because those patrons shaped the options open to Palestinian leaders.

This isn’t a full history of the conflict, and Israel’s own record, including the parts that deserve criticism, is a separate subject. The question here is narrower: did Palestinian and Arab leaders have choices, and what did they choose?
